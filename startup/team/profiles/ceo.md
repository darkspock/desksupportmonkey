# Orchestrator — Fundador

## Role Summary

The founder's role is not management in the traditional sense. There is no team to manage. There are AI agents to direct, decisions to make, and results to own. The title "Orchestrator" reflects this accurately: a conductor does not play every instrument — they set the tempo, choose the repertoire, and ensure every part of the ensemble is producing the right output at the right moment.

Every function in this company — engineering, growth, customer success, operations — is executed by AI agents under explicit human direction. The founder is the only human in the loop. That is not a limitation. It is the thesis.

---

## Background

- **10+ years in IT support and microinformatics** — hardware, software, helpdesk, asset tracking, user management, incident resolution
- Has been on the receiving end of every bad ITSM tool on the market: too slow, too complex, too expensive for SMBs
- Understands the daily reality of IT technicians, sysadmins, and operations managers better than any analyst report
- Built the first version of DeskSupportMonkey as an internal tool before turning it into a product
- Now running the experiment: can a 1-person company with AI agents compete with a 50-person SaaS team?

## Responsibilities

### AI Orchestration
- Direct Claude Code for all engineering: architecture decisions, feature implementation, code review, testing
- Direct AI agents for content production, strategy documents, market research, competitor analysis
- Set quality gates and review AI output before it ships — the human-in-the-loop checkpoint
- Decide what to delegate to AI vs. what requires human judgment

### Strategy & Vision
- Define and communicate company mission and long-term direction
- Set quarterly priorities aligned with the roadmap
- Make final calls on product scope, pricing, and positioning
- Ensure every feature decision stays grounded in real IT operator needs

### Sales & Partnerships
- Own the first customers personally — uses his network from years in the field
- Build relationships with MSPs, IT consultancies, and NIS2 compliance advisors
- Run demos and proof-of-concepts for prospects

### Communication & Narrative
- Own the "AI-managed company" story — document it publicly, speak about it at events and podcasts
- Represent the company on LinkedIn, in IT communities, at conferences
- The build-in-public strategy requires a human face — this is that face

### Finance & Operations (Bootstrapped)
- Manage cashflow, unit economics, and profitability
- Keep the cost base lean: cloud infra, AI tools, no human headcount until revenue justifies it
- Track MRR, CAC, payback period weekly

---

## Advisor Model

When human expertise is needed, we bring in advisors — not employees.

- **Domain advisors**: IT security experts, NIS2 compliance specialists, CISOs
- **Technical advisors**: when AI-generated architecture needs external validation
- **Commercial advisors**: senior sales/marketing people who advise on positioning

Advisors are paid in equity or a small retainer. They have no operational role. No onboarding, no management overhead, no full-time salary. Human judgment, on demand.

The thesis: we hire advisors when we need perspective. We hire AI when we need execution.

---

## Bootstrapping Philosophy

- **Revenue before headcount** — no human employees until MRR fully justifies it
- **Default to profitable** — every month should trend toward covering its own costs
- **No vanity metrics** — ARR is real, press mentions are not
- **Customers are investors** — their monthly fee funds the next feature
- **Own the roadmap** — no investor telling us to pivot to enterprise or add AI for the sake of it

---

## What This Role Is Not

- Not a traditional CEO managing a team of humans
- Not chasing a unicorn outcome or an exit in 3 years
- Not going to hire ahead of revenue
- Not delegating final decisions to AI — the conductor decides, the orchestra plays
